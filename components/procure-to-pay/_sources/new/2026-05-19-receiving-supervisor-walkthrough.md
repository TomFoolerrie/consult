# Walkthrough — Receiving Dock, Plant 2

**Client:** Nordhaven Industrial Group
**Date:** 2026-05-19
**Location:** Plant 2, Rivermouth — inbound dock and receiving office
**Participants:** Hal Ostrowski (Receiving Supervisor, Plant 2)
**Interviewer:** Engagement team
**Format:** Standing walkthrough, ~50 min, notes cleaned up from recording

---

**Interviewer:** Show me a truck arriving.

**Hal Ostrowski:** Alright. Carrier pulls into door four. Driver hands over the bill of lading. First thing my guys do is count pieces against the BOL and eyeball for damage. If the count's short or something's crushed we note it on the BOL, driver signs, we keep a copy and scan it later.

**Interviewer:** Then into the system?

**Hal Ostrowski:** Then we find the PO. Packing slip usually has it. If it doesn't — and that happens more than you'd think, especially the smaller MRO stuff — we've got to hunt. We'll search NetSuite by vendor and by item, Transactions > Purchases > Receive Orders, and look for an open PO with the right quantity. If we can't find one, it goes in the cage.

**Interviewer:** The cage.

**Hal Ostrowski:** [laughs] Yeah. Fenced area, back corner. Anything we can't tie to a PO sits there until somebody claims it. Right now there's — I don't know, thirty pallets? Some of it's been there since last fall. That's usually somebody called a vendor direct without a req.

**Interviewer:** How does the receipt actually get entered?

**Hal Ostrowski:** Receive Orders screen. We pull up the PO, key the quantity received per line, put the packing slip number in the memo field, and save. That creates the item receipt. For anything going to inventory we also print a label and it gets put away by location.

**Interviewer:** Same day?

**Hal Ostrowski:** That's the target. Real answer, on a good day yeah, first shift gets it in before end of shift. On a heavy day, or if we're short — I've got two receivers on first, one on second, and second shift honestly does the physical put-away and leaves the keying for morning. So a Tuesday afternoon truck might not be in the system until Thursday.

**Interviewer:** And AP is waiting on that.

**Hal Ostrowski:** So I've been told. Marisol calls me. Or Priya calls me. I get it. I don't have another person.

**Interviewer:** Partial shipments?

**Hal Ostrowski:** You receive what came. PO stays open for the balance. That's fine. Where it gets ugly is over-shipments — vendor sends a hundred and ten when the PO says a hundred. System will let me receive up to — I want to say ten percent over? There's a tolerance. Above it, it blocks and I have to get the buyer to do a change order.

**Interviewer:** Do you know the tolerance number?

**Hal Ostrowski:** Not exactly. Ten percent sticks in my head. There's a dollar one too. Five hundred? Yusuf would know, or whoever set it up.

**Interviewer:** Services and non-inventory?

**Hal Ostrowski:** Not my dock. If somebody buys a service — a contractor, an engineering study — there's no truck. Somebody in the department has to go into the system and do a receipt against the service PO to say "yes this happened." I know that's the process. I don't know how well it works because I never see it. My guess is not well.

**Interviewer:** What about returns to vendor?

**Hal Ostrowski:** [00:31] If it's damaged or wrong we do an RMA. I email the buyer, buyer gets an RMA number from the vendor, we do a Return Authorization in NetSuite against the receipt, and we ship it back on the vendor's account. Then there's supposed to be a credit memo. Whether the credit ever shows up is not something I track. AP chases that, I think.

**Interviewer:** How do you know if there's a discrepancy after the fact?

**Hal Ostrowski:** Usually because Priya emails me. There's a match exception, the bill says ninety-six and I received a hundred, and we go back through the packing slips. Half the time it's a keying error on my side, half the time it's the vendor. We keep packing slips in the file cabinet in receiving, by month, for — I want to say two years? Then they go to the storage container out back.

**Interviewer:** Are they scanned anywhere?

**Hal Ostrowski:** No. Paper only. Somebody talked about scanning them into the OCR thing but that never happened.

**Interviewer:** Freight bills?

**Hal Ostrowski:** Those go straight to AP, I don't touch them. Unless it's a collect shipment and then there's a whole conversation about who authorized collect.

**Interviewer:** Cycle counts, inventory accuracy — related?

**Hal Ostrowski:** We cycle count A items monthly, B quarterly, C annually. Accuracy's around ninety-six, ninety-seven percent. A chunk of the variance traces back to receipts entered wrong or entered late, so it's the same root cause.

**Interviewer:** If you could fix one thing?

**Hal Ostrowski:** Scanners. Give me handheld scanners that read the vendor's barcode off the packing slip and post the receipt right there at the door. We looked at it two years ago, it was like sixty thousand dollars and it got cut. Second thing — make it impossible for people to call a vendor direct. Everything in the cage is that.

**Interviewer:** Is Plant 1 and Plant 3 the same process?

**Hal Ostrowski:** Plant 1, basically. Plant 3 does something different, they've got a Kanban thing with the two big steel suppliers where the receipt auto-posts off consumption. I don't understand it well enough to explain it. Talk to Yusuf.
