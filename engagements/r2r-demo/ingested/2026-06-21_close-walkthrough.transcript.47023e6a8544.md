# Close Walkthrough

**Associate:** Thanks everyone for joining. Today we're walking through the monthly close for Record to Report. Let's start with how the close kicks off.

**Controller:** Sure. Close starts on business day one. We run the sub-ledger close for AP and AR first, then post accruals manually in the GL. Most of the accrual entries are keyed by hand into the ERP.

**Associate:** So the accruals are a manual journal entry process? No automation there?

**Controller:** Correct, accruals are fully manual today. The team builds them in spreadsheets and re-keys them. It's a real pain point every month and the most common source of late adjustments. After the trial balance is locked we move to account reconciliations. Each balance sheet account owner signs off in our recon tool, but the sign-off is decentralized — every regional controller owns their own accounts.

**Associate:** Got it. And is there a documented control over the recon sign-off?

**Controller:** Yes — we require a second-level reviewer to approve every reconciliation above fifty thousand dollars before the account is closed. That dual-review control is enforced every single close.

**Associate:** Is that dual-review control written down anywhere — a policy document or a system-enforced rule?

**Controller:** Honestly, no. It's how we've always done it but it isn't written into any policy or system config. It lives in people's heads. I can confirm it happens though. Once recons are signed off, we hand the consolidated trial balance to the consolidation team for eliminations and topside entries.

**Associate:** Before we move on — one thing that came up earlier. You mentioned the office lease in the Denver building is up for renewal next quarter and facilities wants finance to weigh in on the square-footage commitment.

**Controller:** Right, the Denver office lease renewal. That's really a facilities and real-estate decision, nothing to do with the close process itself, but they keep looping us in on the negotiation.

**Associate:** Understood, we'll note that separately. Let's keep going with management review.
